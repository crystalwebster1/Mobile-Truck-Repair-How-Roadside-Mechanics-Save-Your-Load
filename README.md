## **Which Problems Can a Mechanic Fix at the Roadside?**

Plenty of common faults are quick wins for a stocked service truck. Air line leaks, dead batteries, failed sensors, split coolant hoses, and blown fuses all fall into that group. Trailer lights and flat tires fit as well, so a single call can cover both the tractor and the box behind it.

### **Faults That Usually Finish on Scene**

*   Air leaks from lines, fittings, and glad hands
*   Weak batteries and corroded ground connections
*   Coolant hose failures and loose belts
*   Sensor faults that trigger a power derate
*   Trailer lighting problems and reefer unit alarms

### **When the Repair Needs a Shop**

## **What Should You Do Before the Technician Arrives?**

A few small actions make the visit smoother and keep you safe while you wait. Follow this short checklist and pass the details to dispatch.

1.  Move the truck as far from traffic as you safely can and switch on your hazard lights.
2.  Set out warning triangles only if it is safe to leave the cab.
3.  Note every dash warning, odd sound, and recent symptom.
4.  Have your engine make, trailer type, and exact location ready.
5.  Keep your paperwork and payment method within reach.

Those details help dispatch match the right crew. A driver who says the air pressure drops after every stop gives the technician a head start, while a driver who only says the truck will not move leaves everyone guessing.
